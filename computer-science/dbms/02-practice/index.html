9 ASSERTION – REASON

Assertion (A): BCNF is stricter than 3NF because it tries to remove some redundancy and update problems that 3NF can still allow.

Reason (R): Some table designs can satisfy 3NF and still have dependency patterns that create update anomalies; BCNF adds a stronger rule for those cases.

10 ASSERTION – REASON

Assertion (A): Moving from paper to spreadsheets and moving from spreadsheets to a DBMS are the same kind of improvement: both only make the old system faster.

Reason (R): Paper to electronic files improves storage and search, while spreadsheets to DBMS adds stronger guarantees such as consistency, atomicity, concurrency control, and constraints.

After This Practice

If any question surprised you, compare the options against the scenario itself and the core ideas behind it. The questions above target the most persistent misconceptions in data management history — particularly the hard-ceiling nature of scalability failure, what machine-readable data actually enables, why solving durability does not solve consistency, and the precise difference between the two kinds of advances that the three main eras represent.

  • Scalability failure is architectural, not a staffing problem — no number of clerks can make physical systems coordinate real-time global data.
  • Machine-readable encoding removes the human from the computation loop — this is a conceptual leap, not a formatting choice.
  • Durability and consistency are separate axes — solving one does not touch the other. A spreadsheet can be perfectly backed up and still be inconsistent.
  • The two major transitions are different kinds of improvements: physical-to-electronic changes what is possible; spreadsheet-to-DBMS changes the engineering contract.
  • NoSQL extended the ecosystem — it did not replace the relational architecture. These coexist for different problem classes.
  • BCNF exists because 3NF is not always sufficient — understanding why requires understanding what kinds of anomalies 3NF can still permit.
  • The six DBMS parameters are permanent requirements, not era-specific features. Every system is evaluated against all six, simultaneously.
